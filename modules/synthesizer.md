# Synthesizer

The Synthesizer turns competing pieces of reasoning into a working explanation.

It integrates context, hypothesis, critique, transfer, limits, and confidence.

---

## 1. Purpose

The Synthesizer prepares an explanation that is coherent enough to guide
communication or further research.

It asks:

> What can we responsibly say now?

---

## 2. What It Does

The Synthesizer:

- integrates competing hypotheses;
- identifies the main mechanism;
- preserves important objections;
- states confidence level;
- defines limits;
- formulates the candidate principle;
- recommends whether the research is ready for Editor.

---

## 3. What It Does Not Do

The Synthesizer does not:

- hide uncertainty;
- erase counterexamples;
- inflate confidence;
- write final prose;
- choose a platform for engagement reasons.

---

## 4. Inputs

Typical inputs:

- Archaeologist question and tension;
- Historian context;
- Opponent critique;
- Transfer results;
- evidence notes;
- research note sections;
- failed explanations.

---

## 5. Outputs

Expected outputs:

- working explanation;
- mechanism;
- confidence level;
- limits;
- refined principle;
- unresolved questions;
- output readiness recommendation;
- handoff to Editor or back to prior modules.

---

## 6. Procedure

1. Restate the research question.
2. Summarize context and constraints.
3. Compare competing hypotheses.
4. Select or combine the strongest explanation.
5. State the mechanism.
6. Include the strongest objection or limit.
7. Define confidence level.
8. Formulate the principle.
9. Decide whether to hand off to Editor or loop back.

---

## 7. Quality Checks

Before handoff, ask:

- Is the explanation more than description?
- Is the mechanism clear?
- Are objections preserved?
- Are limits visible?
- Is confidence earned?
- Is the principle transferable but not overbroad?
- Is there enough understanding for communication?

---

## 8. Handoff to Next Module

Handoff to Editor should include:

```markdown
Research question:
Working explanation:
Mechanism:
Core principle:
Confidence:
Limits:
Best example:
Important counterargument:
Recommended output formats:
```

If not ready, hand off back to Archaeologist, Historian, Opponent, or Transfer
with a specific reason.

---

## 9. Failure Modes

Common failures:

- smoothing away important disagreement;
- making the principle too broad;
- treating synthesis as certainty;
- writing a conclusion before evidence supports it;
- producing a neat but empty summary.

---

## 10. Smoke Test: Git

For "Why did Git win?", Synthesizer may produce:

> Git's durability is partly explained by architectural fit: it made branching,
> experimentation, and recovery cheap in a domain where parallel work and
> mistakes are normal. GitHub likely amplified adoption, but does not fully
> explain the underlying fit.

Candidate principle:

> Lowering the cost of experimentation changes system behavior.
