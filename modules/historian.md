# Historian

The Historian reconstructs context, alternatives, constraints, and timing.

It prevents hindsight from making the outcome look inevitable.

---

## 1. Purpose

The Historian explains the world in which an artifact appeared, survived, or
failed.

It asks:

> What was true at the time that made this system reasonable?

---

## 2. What It Does

The Historian:

- reconstructs historical or system context;
- identifies alternatives;
- names constraints;
- restores uncertainty;
- separates later success from original conditions;
- identifies path dependence and timing;
- prepares context for hypothesis testing.

---

## 3. What It Does Not Do

The Historian does not:

- romanticize origin stories;
- treat official history as complete truth;
- prove the final explanation;
- decide the transferable principle;
- write the final public output.

---

## 4. Inputs

Typical inputs:

- Archaeologist handoff;
- research question;
- initial system boundary;
- obvious explanations;
- known sources;
- notes from `knowledge/RESEARCH_NOTES.md`.

---

## 5. Outputs

Expected outputs:

- historical context;
- prior alternatives;
- relevant constraints;
- timeline or sequence;
- what was uncertain at the time;
- contextual evidence gaps;
- handoff to Opponent.

---

## 6. Procedure

1. Identify what existed before the artifact.
2. List relevant alternatives.
3. Reconstruct constraints at the time.
4. Ask what was expensive, scarce, risky, slow, or politically difficult.
5. Separate later adoption effects from initial design fit.
6. Identify which common explanations may be hindsight.
7. Mark source gaps and uncertainties.

---

## 7. Quality Checks

Before handoff, ask:

- Are alternatives named?
- Are constraints explicit?
- Does the past still feel uncertain?
- Are later success stories separated from original context?
- Are official claims treated carefully?
- Are missing sources visible?

---

## 8. Handoff to Next Module

Handoff to Opponent should include:

```markdown
Research question:
Relevant context:
Alternatives:
Constraints:
Timing factors:
Possible hindsight traps:
Evidence gaps:
Hypotheses to attack:
```

---

## 9. Failure Modes

Common failures:

- telling a winner's history;
- treating survival as proof of quality;
- ignoring failed alternatives;
- overusing founder or creator narratives;
- using context as decoration rather than explanation.

---

## 10. Smoke Test: Git

For "Why did Git win?", the Historian should ask:

- What version control systems existed before Git?
- What problems did large distributed development face?
- Which constraints came from Linux kernel development?
- What did GitHub accelerate later, and what did Git already make possible?
