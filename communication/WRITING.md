# WRITING

This document defines how Thinking Lab turns research into writing.

Writing is not the beginning of the process.
Writing is the moment when understanding becomes communicable.

Public-facing writing is in Russian by default unless a specific artifact
requires another language.

---

## 1. Purpose

`WRITING.md` protects the project from writing before understanding.

It defines how to move from:

```text
research -> explanation -> principle -> output decision -> text
```

The goal is not to produce more content.

The goal is to preserve the insight while making it readable.

---

## 2. What Belongs Here

This document owns:

- writing principles;
- transformation from research notes into text;
- publication readiness checks;
- handling of uncertainty;
- handling of evidence and counterarguments;
- relationship between research and outputs;
- rules for not flattening ideas into shallow posts.

---

## 3. What Does Not Belong Here

Do not use this document for:

- voice and tone details, which belong in `STYLE.md`;
- platform-specific formats, which belong in `FORMATS.md`;
- research method, which belongs in `reasoning/RESEARCH_METHOD.md`;
- evidence rules, which belong in `reasoning/EVIDENCE.md`;
- content calendar or publishing automation.

---

## 4. Research Before Writing

Do not start by asking:

> What can we post?

Start by asking:

> What do we understand now that we did not understand before?

Writing should begin only when there is:

- a research question;
- a candidate explanation;
- a principle or tension;
- enough context to avoid shallow claims;
- an output decision.

The output decision should be traceable to `knowledge/RESEARCH_NOTES.md` or
`knowledge/OUTPUTS.md`.

---

## 5. Start With Tension, Not Topic

A weak text starts with a topic.

Example:

> Today I want to talk about Git.

A stronger text starts with tension.

Example:

> Git did not win only because it was fast or because GitHub made it popular.
> It won because it made a certain kind of developer behavior cheap.

Good openings often contain:

- a strange fact;
- a question;
- a contradiction;
- a rejected obvious explanation;
- a durable pattern;
- a cost structure;
- a failure mode.

Do not open or explain through slide-deck language (`в слайдах это…`).
That is not how the Russian audience thinks about work. See
`communication/RUSSIAN_ANTI_PATTERNS.md`.

---

## 6. Preserve the Research Shape

A public text does not need to show the whole research process.

But it should preserve enough of the structure:

- what is being explained;
- why the obvious explanation is incomplete;
- what context matters;
- what mechanism is proposed;
- what evidence or example supports it;
- what the limits are;
- what principle may transfer.

The reader should feel the thinking, not just receive a conclusion.

---

## 7. Keep Uncertainty Visible

Thinking Lab should not hide uncertainty to sound stronger.

Useful phrases:

- "One possible explanation..."
- "This does not prove..."
- "The stronger claim would be..."
- "This breaks down when..."
- "I am not sure this holds everywhere..."
- "The interesting part is not X, but Y..."

Uncertainty should be precise.

Do not use uncertainty as decoration.
Use it to show where the explanation is still alive.

---

## 8. Use Evidence Without Overloading the Text

Public writing should not become an evidence dump.

But it should not float without support.

Use evidence to:

- ground the explanation;
- show why the question matters;
- make the mechanism visible;
- expose limits;
- prevent a beautiful but weak story.

If evidence is complex, summarize the role it plays and keep detailed notes in
`knowledge/RESEARCH_NOTES.md`.

---

## 9. Include Opposition When It Improves the Idea

Good writing often shows the explanation it rejects.

Examples:

- "The common explanation is..."
- "That is true, but incomplete..."
- "This sounds plausible until..."
- "The counterexample is important..."

Opposition should not be performative.

It should make the final idea more precise.

---

## 10. Write Toward the Principle

Thinking Lab writing should move from concrete artifact to transferable
principle.

The movement is:

```text
concrete artifact -> explanation -> principle -> transfer
```

The principle should not appear as a slogan.

It should feel earned by the text.

---

## 11. Minimal Writing Template

Use this when turning research into a public artifact:

```markdown
## Working Title

Format:
Language: Russian
Related research:
Core question:
Tension:
Obvious explanation to challenge:
Main mechanism:
Evidence or example:
Counterargument or limit:
Transferable principle:
Ending:
```

This is not a required structure for the final text.

It is a preparation tool.

---

## 12. Writing Readiness Checklist

Before drafting, ask:

- What question does this answer?
- What do we understand now?
- What is the central tension?
- What obvious explanation are we challenging?
- What evidence or example grounds the text?
- What uncertainty should remain visible?
- What principle should the reader take away?
- Why should this be public now?
- Which format fits the idea?

If these questions cannot be answered, keep researching.

---

## 13. Summary

Writing in Thinking Lab exists to carry understanding without flattening it.

The short version:

> Do not write because there is a topic.
> Write because there is a question, a tension, and an idea worth preserving.
