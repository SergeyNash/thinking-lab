# ANALOGIES

This document stores cross-domain analogies.

Analogies are treated as hypotheses, not conclusions.

---

## 1. Purpose

`ANALOGIES.md` helps Thinking Lab remember potentially useful transfers between
domains.

An analogy belongs here when it may reveal a shared structure behind different
surface phenomena.

---

## 2. What Belongs Here

Add analogies that include:

- source domain;
- target domain;
- shared structure;
- what the analogy reveals;
- where it breaks;
- what evidence would strengthen it.

---

## 3. What Does Not Belong Here

Do not add:

- clever metaphors with no research value;
- analogies used only for style;
- comparisons with no shared mechanism;
- claims that treat analogy as proof.

If an analogy becomes a public writing device, it may also appear in
`OUTPUTS.md`.

---

---

## 5. Relationship to Reasoning

Analogies must follow:

- `identity/PHILOSOPHY.md`: analogies are useful and dangerous.
- `reasoning/EPISTEMOLOGY.md`: analogies generate hypotheses.
- `reasoning/EVIDENCE.md`: analogies are not evidence by themselves.

---

## 6. Seed Analogy

## Git and Organizational Experimentation

Status: active  
Source domain: software version control  
Target domain: organizational process design, product experimentation, security,
AI workflows  
Shared structure: lowering the cost of experimentation and recovery can change
how safely a system explores alternatives.  
What it reveals: teams may need organizational equivalents of cheap branching,
review, rollback, history, and evaluation.  
Where it breaks: organizations involve politics, emotions, incentives, and
power structures that do not map cleanly to technical systems. AI workflows may
make generation cheap while keeping verification expensive.  
Evidence needed: cases where reversible process experiments, paved roads, or
AI evaluation workflows changed behavior.  
Related research: Why did Git win?
