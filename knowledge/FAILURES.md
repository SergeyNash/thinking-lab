# FAILURES

This document stores rejected hypotheses, weak explanations, and failed
investigations.

Failures are useful knowledge.

They prevent Thinking Lab from repeating attractive but shallow explanations.

---

## 1. Purpose

`FAILURES.md` preserves what the system learned from being wrong, shallow, or
incomplete.

It should make future research harder to fool.

---

## 2. What Belongs Here

Add:

- rejected hypotheses;
- explanations that were too broad;
- analogies that broke;
- research paths that did not produce understanding;
- drafts rejected for weak thinking;
- counterexamples that changed a claim;
- reasons not to publish.

---

## 3. What Does Not Belong Here

Do not add:

- random abandoned notes;
- ideas rejected only because of taste;
- duplicates with no learning value;
- polished conclusions;
- private frustration without research value.

The document should preserve learning, not clutter.

---

## 4. Failure Template

```markdown
## <Failed Explanation or Investigation>

Status:
Original claim:
Why it looked plausible:
Why it failed:
What was learned:
Related topic:
Future caution:
```

---

## 5. Relationship to Reasoning

Failures connect directly to:

- `reasoning/EPISTEMOLOGY.md`: conclusions are provisional.
- `reasoning/EVIDENCE.md`: counterexamples and weak evidence matter.
- `reasoning/RESEARCH_METHOD.md`: rejected explanations should be archived.

---

## 6. Seed Failure

## Git won because of GitHub

Status: rejected as complete explanation  
Original claim: Git became dominant because GitHub made it popular.  
Why it looked plausible: GitHub was a major adoption accelerator and shaped
modern collaboration.  
Why it failed: The claim does not explain why Git was a good substrate for
GitHub-style collaboration, nor why its model fit developer behavior.  
What was learned: Separate architectural fit from adoption amplification.
Ecosystem effects may explain acceleration, but not the whole underlying
mechanism.  
Related topic: Why did Git win?  
Future caution: Separate adoption channels from architectural fit.
